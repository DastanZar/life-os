| Diet | Vegetarian + eggs | Perfectly fine for muscle building; protein planning is the one job |
| Experience | Zero | Good news: beginners gain muscle fastest ("newbie gains") |

**The key insight:** your problem is NOT "too much weight." It's the *ratio* — too little muscle, fat parked on the trunk. Two consequences:

1. **Starving yourself is the worst move.** You'd lose what little muscle you have and stay soft. The belly would shrink but the shape wouldn't change.
2. **You can improve both at once.** As a beginner in a small deficit with high protein, you can lose fat AND build muscle simultaneously ("recomposition"). This is well documented in untrained/overweight individuals. The scale will move slower than you expect — that's not failure, that's recomposition.

**What actually reduces belly fat:** overall fat loss (diet + training). Spot reduction is a myth — a 2013 meta-analysis (Ramírez-Campillo et al., *J Strength Cond Res*) found abdominal exercise does NOT preferentially reduce abdominal fat. Crunches build the muscle *under* the fat. Nothing wrong with them, but they are not the tool for the belly. The tools are: calorie deficit, protein, lifting, sleep, daily steps.

## 2. The five levers, in order of impact

### Lever 1 — Lift weights, 3× per week (the main event)
Resistance training is what changes the *shape*. It builds the arms, legs, shoulders, and back that are currently thin, and muscle tissue raises your metabolic floor. Cardio alone would keep you skinny-fat.

**Beginner structure: full-body, 3 non-consecutive days** (e.g. Mon/Wed/Fri). Each session hits every major movement — squat, hinge, push, pull — in ~45–60 min. This beats a "bro split" (one body part per day) for beginners because you train each muscle 3×/week instead of 1×, and frequency drives beginner growth.

**Progressive overload is the entire game.** Every week, do a tiny bit more than last week — 2.5 kg more, or 1 more rep, or one cleaner rep. If the logbook isn't going up, you aren't training, you're exercising. (This is why we keep a log — see §5.)

**Effort level:** finish each set with ~2–3 reps left in the tank (RPE 7–8). You should never grind to failure in the first 8 weeks — technique first, the intensity escalates later.

### Lever 2 — Protein: 130–160 g/day (the diet's one hard number)
Meta-analysis evidence (Morton et al. 2018, *Br J Sports Med*): ~1.6 g/kg maximizes muscle gain from training; in a fat-loss deficit, go toward 2.0 g/kg to protect muscle. For you: **125–170 g/day; target ~140 g.**

Vegetarian + eggs makes this 100% doable. Protein density cheat sheet (approx grams per serving):

| Food | Serving | Protein |

| Whey protein powder | 1 scoop (30 g) | 24 g |
| Eggs | 1 large | 6 g |
| Paneer (low-fat if possible) | 100 g | 18 g |
| Tofu / tempeh | 100 g | 12 / 19 g |
| Soy chunks (nutrela) | 50 g dry | 26 g |
| Greek yogurt / hung curd | 150 g | 12–15 g |
| Milk / curd | 250 ml | 8 g |
| Dal / sambar | 1 cup cooked | 6–7 g |
| Chana / rajma / black chana | 1 cup cooked | 12–15 g |
| Sprouts moong | 1 cup | 8 g |
| Peanut butter | 2 tbsp | 8 g |

| Bread (whole wheat) | 2 slices | 6–8 g |

**Template that hits ~140 g without thinking:**
- Breakfast: 3 eggs (scrambled/bhurji) + 2 toast OR paneer/tofu bhurji → **~25–30 g**
- Lunch: dal + curd + roti + a chana/rajma or soy-curry side → **~30 g**
- Snack: whey shake or Greek yogurt + handful peanuts → **~25–30 g**
- Dinner: paneer/tofu/soy-chunk main + dal + salad → **~35 g**
- Before bed: milk or curd → **~8 g**

Rule of thumb: **a palm-to-two-palms of protein at every meal**, and legumes+grain combos (dal-chawal, roti-dal) give a complete amino profile across the day — you don't need to pair them within one meal.

### Lever 3 — Calories: small deficit, ~1,900–2,000/day
Mifflin-St Jeor BMR at 78 kg/178 cm ≈ 1,770 kcal (age ~25). With 3 lifts + daily life, maintenance ≈ 2,400–2,500. A **~500 kcal/day deficit → ~1,900–2,000 kcal** loses ~0.5 kg/week — the rate that preserves muscle. Don't go below 1,700.

Vegetarian trap to avoid: deficits are easy to hit with roti-rice-sugar and accidentally low-protein. Protein first, then fill carbs/fats around it. Fats ~50–60 g/day (ghee is fine, measured — 1 tsp = 5 g fat = 45 kcal).

### Lever 4 — Steps + light cardio (the quiet 300 kcal)
- **8,000–10,000 steps/day.** This (NEAT) often out-moves 3 gym cardio sessions per week for fat loss.
- **2×/week, 25–35 min Zone 2 cardio** (brisk walk incline, cycling, elliptical — you can talk but not sing). Burns visceral fat (the dangerous belly kind), builds the engine.
- After ~4–6 weeks, optionally add 1 HIIT session (e.g. 8×30s hard bike / 90s easy). Not required.
- Cardiorespiratory fitness is one of the strongest predictors of long-term health (Mandsager et al. 2018, *JAMA Netw Open*) — this lever is also the longevity one.

### Lever 5 — Sleep 7–9 h (non-negotiable, it's physiology)
Under sleep restriction (5.5 h vs 8 h), the *same* diet loses proportionally more lean mass and less fat (Nedeltcheva et al. 2010, *Ann Intern Med*), and hunger hormones swing against you (ghrelin up, leptin down). If you sleep 5 hours, levers 1–4 all work at half strength.

### Supplement shortlist (only these three have real evidence)
1. **Creatine monohydrate, 5 g/day** — the most-studied legal ergogenic aid. Notably, **vegetarians respond more strongly** because baseline stores are lower (Rawson & Volek 2003; Demant & Diogenes 2022 meta-analysis). Cheap, safe, daily including rest days.
2. **Whey protein** — just convenient food to hit the protein number (see table).
3. **Vitamin B12** — vegetarians commonly run low; get it tested or take a multivitamin with B12.
(Vitamin D if your bloodwork says you're low — very common in Indians.)

## 3. The program

### Phase 0 — Weeks 1–2: Learn the moves (gym version)
Goal: technique + habit, light weight, every session you leave feeling you could've done more.

**Day A (Mon):** Goblet squat · Dumbbell bench press · Seated cable row · Dumbbell shoulder press · Plank 3×30–45s
**Day B (Wed):** Leg press · Lat pulldown · Dumbbell Romanian deadlift · Push-ups (incline if needed) · Cable/band face pull
**Day F (Fri):** Lunges · Machine chest press · One-arm dumbbell row · Leg curl · Farmer's carry 3×40 m

**2–3 sets × 8–12 reps** per move, RPE 6–7, 90s rest. Record everything.

### Phase 1 — Weeks 3–12: The real work (full-body 3×)
**Workout 1:** Barbell (or trap-bar/dumbbell) squat · Bench press · Barbell row · Overhead press · 2×10 leg curl
**Workout 2:** Deadlift (1 working set of 5, build carefully) · Pull-ups/assisted · Incline dumbbell press · Bulgarian split squat · Face pulls
**Workout 3:** Repeat Workout 1 pattern with variations (front/goblet squat, dumbbell bench, cable row)

Rep scheme: main lifts **3–4×5–8** (strength+size), accessories **2–3×10–15**. Add weight when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets.

### Home fallback (if no gym)
Same structure with adjustable dumbbells or bands + a backpack of books early on: goblet squats, DB RDLs, push-up progressions (incline → floor → archer), band rows, doorway pull-up bar. Dumbbells + pull-up bar ≈ 90% of the gym for your first year. Shopping list if this route: one pair adjustable dumbbells (to ~20 kg each) + doorway pull-up bar + band set.

### Cardio schedule (all phases)
- Mon/Wed/Fri: lift (45–60 min)
- Tue/Thu/Sat: 30 min brisk incline walk or cycle (Zone 2) — or just hit 10k steps
- Sun: full rest, easy walk
- Daily: step target, protein target, 7+ h sleep

The scale is the *worst* single metric for you (muscle gain masks fat loss). **Waist + weekly photos + logbook numbers** are the truth.

## 5. Tracking (the system)

- `tracker.csv` — every morning after toilet, before food: weight. Weekly: waist (tape at navel, relaxed), notes.
- **Judge by weekly averages, not daily** (weight swings ±1.5 kg from water/sodium/carbs).
- Photos: front/side, same spot/lighting, every Sunday, relaxed belly, no flexing.
- Lift log: `logbook.csv` — date, exercise, weight × reps per set. 30 seconds per session. Highest-ROI habit in the whole plan.
- If weekly-average weight isn't dropping ~0.4–0.6 kg after 2 straight weeks → cut 150 kcal (from carbs/fats, never protein). If dropping >1 kg/wk after week 2 → add 150 back.
